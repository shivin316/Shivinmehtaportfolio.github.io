Senior Business Analyst — Product Quality & Personalisation
Jan 2026 – Present
Meesho · Bangalore, India

The What

Own the quality and personalisation layer of a marketplace serving hundreds of millions of users — redesigning how products are ranked and recommended so buyers see higher-quality items first and sellers get rewarded for quality.

The How

  • Rebuilt the product ranking algorithm to incorporate a per-user quality signal, modelled from the share of negative ratings a product accumulates — a lightweight, interpretable risk score that updates continuously.
  • Built a personalisation layer using a content-based recommender over product attributes (size, colour, material) that users signal preference for through pre-purchase behaviour.
  • Applied computer vision / multimedia quality signals (via adoption of user-submitted photos and videos) to enrich the item representation and improve buyers' pre-purchase confidence.
  • Ran counterfactual and holdout experiments to isolate the causal effect of each change — separating ranking signal lift from seasonality and catalogue mix.
Ranking algorithms Recommender systems Counterfactual analysis Holdout experiments Behavioural signals
Impact: Cut the platform-wide negative-rating rate by 0.8 pp via the ranking redesign, a further 0.4 pp via personalised feeds, and 1 pp more through user-generated media — a compounding ~2.2 pp quality improvement equivalent to 15 % drop in negative ratings across the marketplace, one of the largest single-team quality wins of the year.
🧠 Me: "Let's add a quality signal to the ranking." Product: "Will it hurt GMV?" Me: "No, it'll help both quality and GMV." Product: "Cool, ship it." — this conversation happens approximately once per quarter, and I live for it.
Business Analyst II — Heavyweight Pricing, Discovery & Logistics
Jan 2025 – Dec 2025
Meesho · Bangalore, India

The What

Led analytics for the 0-to-1 charter of Heavyweight products (5 kg+) — building the pricing, discovery, and logistics foundation for a category that didn't exist before. Think furniture, large appliances, and everything that breaks your back and your delivery SLA.

The How

  • Designed and ran discovery experiments to grow the category from 0 to over 1% of total orders, using uplift modelling to prioritise which interventions actually mattered.
  • Built a product-name-based weight extraction model — a text-mining + regression pipeline that predicts shipping weight from unstructured product titles, since the catalogue weight field was 3–40% accurate at best.
  • Applied unsupervised anomaly detection (clustering + behavioural fingerprinting) to identify fraudulent users and low-performing delivery areas, then restricted their product visibility — a targeted intervention that cut return-to-origin (RTO) incidents sharply.
  • Ran deep dives into central pricing models using regression analysis and leakage diagnostics, surfacing structural pricing leaks that drove org-wide fixes.
  • Built a central analytics dashboard and single-source-of-truth data table (dimensional model in Snowflake/Databricks) that became the category team's default reference.
Uplift modelling NLP for feature extraction Clustering & anomaly detection Regression analysis Dimensional modelling
Impact: Grew Heavyweight's share of total platform NMV by 0.7 pp, equivalent to 88% increase and proved PMF in Large Furniture product category; lifted weight accuracy from 3–40% → 70%+; reduced failed-delivery returns (RTO) by 5 pp, equivalent to 40% drop through fraud detection; and triggered organisation-wide fixes on pricing leakages.
📦 Building a weight-prediction model from product titles is peak "the data doesn't exist, so I'll create it" energy. Also: the number of sellers who write "1kg" in a 40kg sofa listing is not zero, and I have the receipts.
